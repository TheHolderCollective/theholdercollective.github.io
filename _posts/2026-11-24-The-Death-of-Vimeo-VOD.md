Last week Friday I discovered, after having recommended Vimeo to a friend for the promotion of their short films, that Vimeo On Demand(VOD) was being phased out. I discovered this after noticing that the option to rent or buy films had been disabled. At first I thought that maybe the creator had been censored in some way, but after seeing that it was so for other film makers I clicked on one of the suggested links to find out more, and was greeted with the bad news. Vimeo On Demand was being shutdown, and that same Friday (20th November) was going to be the last day my purchases were going to be available for viewing and possible download if that option came with the video. I was somewhat distraught to say the least. It felt as though a friend had died.

![VOD-Shutdown-Info](assets/posts/20261124/VOD-Shutdown-Info.jpg){: width="1861" height="873"}
_VOD Shutdown Information_

The last time I had seriously used the service was in March when I had purchased and rented some of the best chess training content you'll ever come across at a good price: Roman's Chess Lab. While I never binged Vimeo content like I did at one point Youtube content, I had a great appreciation for what the VOD service gave. The films were done by mostly independent film makers not financed by big media houses, and I was able to enjoy films on interesting topics such as elephant conservation, life after the 2004 tsunami in Sri Lanka, alternative Earth pre-histories, UFOs, immigrant worker experiences, and rats. Yes, I said rats. There was a film called "Rat Film" by Theo Anthony which I really enjoyed. I also watched an interesting low budget film called "Blow Up My Life" through the service.

![Sample-Films](assets/posts/20261124/Films-Sample.jpg){: width="905" height="662"}
_Some now inaccessible films which I watched_


I appreciated the relaxed, unfrenzied feel which came along with streaming videos on Vimeo. There was no jostling for recognition by an algorithm by film makers/creators. There was just the content which was left to speak for itself. For me the only downside was that a few films I wanted to watch weren't available in my geographical region, so I could neither rent nor buy them. I'll have to see if I'll be able to access the same creators or content with the new OTT service that Vimeo has phased in. So far I have no idea how to find the creators who used the VOD service on the OTT service. It seems like such a needless change, but I learnt that Vimeo had been acquired by a company last year whose MO is to take good companies and make them worse in the name of profit. So I guess the death of VOD is a consequence of the new owner's guiding philosophy. 

There seem to be alternatives to VOD already brewing, so we'll see what the next few months will bring. VOD was good while it lasted. I have fond memories of watching a short film or two sometimes on Saturday evenings. However, all good things eventually end, so rest in peace VOD.
